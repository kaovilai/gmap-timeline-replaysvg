Timeline replay map

Your Google Maps Timeline, drawn as a city-level world map SVG for a profile README — a still map, a glowing route, or a spinning globe.

  • Private by design
  • City-level output
  • Works offline

1 Pick your export

Timeline.json (Android), location-history.json (iPhone), a Takeout .json, or the .zip the Maps app saves. A places.json this tool produced works too, so you can re-render without the original export. You can also drop the file here.

Where is the export?

Android

  1. Open the phone's Settings app.
  2. Location → Location services → Timeline.
  3. Under “Timeline”, tap Export timeline data.
Google's Android steps

iPhone & iPad

  1. Open Google Maps → profile picture → Settings → Location & Privacy.
  2. Tap Export Timeline data.
  3. On the share sheet, Save to Files → location-history.json.
Open Google Maps Google's iPhone steps

Older Takeout archive

If your history predates the on-device move, Takeout may still have Records.json, Location History.json or Semantic Location History/.

Open Google Takeout

Google publishes no link that opens the Timeline export screen directly — Android's system settings can't be opened from a web page and iOS has no settings URL — so these buttons open the right app or service and the steps above pick it up from there.

          What is in the files
          
    
            Every sample in the export is snapped to the nearest populated place
            in a bundled gazetteer. The output contains the gazetteer's city
            name, region, country, city-centre coordinates, visit counts and
            first/last year. Raw positions, addresses and place names/IDs never
            reach the files.
          
    
        
    

        
    
          Using the app offline
          
    
            Install it from your browser's menu (Add to Home Screen
            on a phone, Install app on desktop) and it opens like
            a native app, with no network needed after the first visit. When you
            publish new files, open the app and reload — the next load fetches
            the new version, and a banner offers a one-tap reload if an update
            lands while you are using it.
